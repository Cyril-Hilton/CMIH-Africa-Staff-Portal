# Client Relations focus rate and Finance dashboard visibility

Focus Rate is distinct accounts engaged in the selected reporting period divided by the full matching All Accounts portfolio, rounded to one decimal place. Six active accounts out of 81 total accounts produces 7.4%. The existing engagement sources are reused; multiple projects for the same normalised account count once. Paging or switching between Engaged and All Accounts does not change the denominator. Period, account, owner and search filters follow the existing account projection. An empty portfolio remains unmeasured; a portfolio with no engagement shows 0%.

The rate appears in a teal badge immediately beside the Engaged This Period heading and count, with its active/total account figures. The existing top financial cards remain intact. The badge wraps on smaller screens and uses the dashboard's existing theme variables.

All active internal staff can now view the published Finance dashboard, both in weekly departmental reports and at the Finance workspace. A separate dashboard-view permission keeps the existing private Finance permission unchanged: this does not grant Operations unit-rate editing, private reimbursements, Finance editing or approval authority. Non-Finance viewers see reviewed native Finance entries; their own reimbursement and supplier submission tools retain their original scope. Payroll continues to show group reporting rather than individual salary/bank/payslip details. Precious's approval authority and Finance publishing workflow remain unchanged. Inactive/service/field-only accounts do not gain this internal dashboard access.

Tests cover the 81/6 example, duplicate account names and projects, owner/search/period changes, empty periods, pagination and the exact badge placement. Finance checks cover the other five departments across all eight tabs, published-versus-pending visibility, no entry controls, denied write/edit attempts, and inactive/service accounts. The account-coverage audit now verifies shared Finance visibility while retaining its write-control and HR privacy assertions.
